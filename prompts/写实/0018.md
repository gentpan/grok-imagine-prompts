## English

**I don’t mean in the**

I don’t mean in the slightest to endorse the sly view so popular during the Cold War that Human Rights would have to wait (i.e., didn’t apply and could be crushed) until we first provided the poor with the basics which, naturally, never actually came to pass, so the day people could finally enjoy Human Rights never came to pass either—on both sides in the Cold War. The rights to the basics and Human Rights apply to all members of the community RIGHT NOW. But we’re still full of excuses about why the time still isn’t right. I know it isn’t easy and I don’t claim to know how to get there but I do claim to know the first step: a commitment by the community to get there. I.e., what Crowley calls an Oath and I prefer to call a pledge, commitment, resolution, i.e., an M engineering resolve of sufficient depth and power to modify the structure of M, to create a new M law that binds the oath-taker, the community in this case.
